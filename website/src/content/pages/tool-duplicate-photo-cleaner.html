Where iPhone duplicate photos actually come from

Nobody takes the same photo twice on purpose. Duplicates are created by the machinery around your library, and once you know the six usual sources, a library full of them stops being mysterious.

  • Backup restores. Restoring from an iCloud or computer backup can re-import photos that were already in the library, producing byte-identical copies.
  • iCloud sync conflicts. Turning iCloud Photos off and on again, or moving between devices, can leave the service holding two records of the same asset.
  • Messaging apps. WhatsApp, Telegram and others auto-save received images into your library, including photos you took yourself and sent.
  • Editing apps. Exporting from an editor often saves a new file next to the original rather than replacing it.
  • Older HDR behaviour. iPhones before the XS could save both the standard and the merged HDR exposure of a shot.
  • Third-party downloads. Anything that saves images — a scanner, a browser, a social app — can add a second copy of a photo you already had.

A five-year-old library has usually accumulated duplicates from three or more of those sources, which is why manual cleanup feels endless: you are looking for a needle in a haystack that keeps refilling.

Exact duplicates versus similar photos

Two photos can look the same and be different files, and the distinction decides how confident you are allowed to be.

Exact duplicates

Identical file data. TidyByte streams each file through SHA-256 and pairs only true matches, so a “duplicate” is not a guess based on file name or date. These are always safe to collapse to one copy.

Visually similar

Different files of the same moment: the same shot re-saved at another size, or two frames from a burst. TidyByte compares Apple Vision feature prints, then shows each group so you can choose the keeper.

Because the two cases carry different risk, they are separate tools in the app. Clear the exact duplicates without thinking, then take a minute over the similar shots. The combined view, when you want it, lists both without double-counting an asset.

How the review screen keeps you out of trouble

  1. Groups, not a list. Each duplicate set is shown together with thumbnails and metadata, so you are deciding within a group rather than scrolling a flat list.
  2. A recommended keeper. Resolution, file size and favourite status pick the best copy, and you can override it with a tap.
  3. Per-group control. Delete one group, select everything, or skip the groups you are unsure about. There is no all-or-nothing button.
  4. A single confirmation. Deletion runs as one Photos change request, so iOS asks once and the operation is atomic.
  5. A 30-day undo. Everything removed goes to Recently Deleted, exactly as if you had deleted it in Photos.

One honest caveat: photos that exist only in iCloud take time to download, so the scan reports how many it skipped instead of silently stalling. If a large share of your library is cloud-only, downloading it first gives a more complete scan.
