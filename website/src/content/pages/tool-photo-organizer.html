Organizing is deciding, not dragging

Most photo-organizing advice assumes you will build the perfect structure: named albums, folders by year, tags for every person. In practice, that advice fails at the same point for almost everyone, because a structure is only useful if the effort of maintaining it is smaller than the effort of finding things without it.

TidyByte takes the opposite approach. Organizing is a sequence of small decisions: keep this, delete that, put this one in an album. Made quickly, one photo at a time, those decisions reduce a library faster than any folder scheme, and the only structure you have to maintain is the albums you already cared about.

Nothing is renamed, moved or re-imported. Your library stays exactly where iOS expects it, so Photos, backups and other apps keep working.

Three ways into a large library

  1. By category

    Every photo is classified on-device with Apple Vision: screenshots, documents, receipts, food, pets, people, and so on. Pick a category and review just those. It is the fastest way to work through a specific kind of clutter without scrolling past everything else.

  2. By year

    The storage breakdown drills into years, and any year opens as a swipe session. Old years are the easiest to clean because the attachment is lowest and the volume is highest.

  3. By album

    Albums you already keep — trips, family, work — open as decks too. Adding to an album is one of the three swipe directions, so filing and deciding happen in the same gesture.

Why swiping works when grids do not

A grid invites browsing. A single card invites a decision. That is the whole trick: by showing one photo at a time, with keep and delete as the only two easy options, the app converts a vague intention (“I should clean up my photos”) into a stream of small, finished actions.

  • Swipe right to keep, left to delete, up to add to an album.
  • Undo the last decision if you change your mind, all the way back to the start of the session.
  • Nothing is deleted during the session. Choices collect on a review screen, and the deletion happens once at the end.
  • Skip whole sessions if you are not in the mood; your progress on individual photos is remembered.

Sessions are bounded, so you can clean a few hundred photos in ten minutes and stop. The app remembers what you have already swiped, so nothing comes back for a second opinion unless you ask it to.

Where to start organizing

If the library feels too large to start, begin with the categories that carry no emotion. They are the ones where you will make the fastest progress and see the clearest result.

  • Screenshots first: high volume, low sentiment.
  • Duplicates second: no judgement required.
  • Blurry and dark shots third: quick decisions, visible progress.
  • Then the swipe deck on the oldest year you have, which is where the volume is.

After one pass, most people find that the library is small enough to browse again — which is the actual goal of organizing.
