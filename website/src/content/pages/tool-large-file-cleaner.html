The arithmetic that explains your storage

A photo is usually one to four megabytes. A minute of 4K video at 60 fps is roughly 350 to 400 MB, and about half that at 30 fps. ProRAW photos land around 25 MB each, and a two-minute screen recording can pass 150 MB.

Put those numbers side by side and the conclusion is unavoidable: you can delete two thousand photos and free less space than deleting six long videos. Any cleanup that ignores file size is working on the wrong end of the problem, which is why finding your largest files first is the highest-yield move available.

Roughly, per item

  • Photo (HEIC): 1–4 MB
  • ProRAW photo: 20–30 MB
  • Live Photo: about double a still
  • Screenshot: 1–3 MB
  • Screen recording: 50–200 MB per couple of minutes
  • 4K video: 350–400 MB per minute at 60 fps

Sorting your library by size

The Large Files tool lists the biggest items in your photo library with their real sizes and lets you sort by size or date. A threshold control sets the floor, so you can look at everything above 50 MB when you have time, or everything above 500 MB when you have two minutes.

  1. Sort by size, largest first. The top ten items are usually where the gigabytes are.
  2. Decide per file. Export the ones worth keeping somewhere else, or compress them in place.
  3. Share before deleting. Multi-select export writes the originals to a folder you choose, so nothing valuable is lost when the file leaves your library.
  4. Compress rather than delete for clips you want to keep at full length but do not need at full bitrate.

Files whose size cannot be read, which usually means they are still in iCloud, are counted separately rather than being reported as zero bytes, so the totals you see are honest.

Delete or compress? A simple rule

Delete it

You will not watch it again: an accidental recording, a clip already backed up elsewhere, a long video of an event you have photos of. Deletion returns the full file size and costs you nothing.

Compress it

You want to keep it, but not at 4K: birthday videos, travel footage, anything you might share. Re-encoding typically cuts a file to a fraction of its size while looking identical on a phone screen.

TidyByte does both from the same list, which is the point: you see the file, you see its size, and you decide whether it stays big, gets smaller, or goes.

Where large files usually hide

  • Slowed-down and time-lapse clips. Slo-mo footage records at high frame rates, so a few seconds can be large.
  • Screen recordings. Frequently forgotten, rarely watched, and often minutes long.
  • Imported camera footage. Anything copied from a DSLR or drone arrives at full bitrate.
  • Old messages media. Videos saved from chat apps keep their original size.
  • Duplicated large videos. A restore can leave you with two copies of the biggest file you own.

Run this tool first on any library that has never been cleaned. Nothing else returns as much space per minute of attention.
