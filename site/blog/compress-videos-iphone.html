How to Compress Videos on iPhone to Save Space

iPhone videos taking up too much storage? Here is everything you need to know about compressing videos on your iPhone — how much space you can save, which method works best, and how to do it without losing noticeable quality.

Why iPhone Videos Take Up So Much Space

Modern iPhones are powerful video cameras, but that power comes with a cost: enormous file sizes. Understanding why your videos are so large is the first step to reclaiming your storage.

4K Resolution

Since the iPhone 6s, every iPhone can record 4K video (3840 x 2160 pixels). That is four times the pixel count of 1080p. A single minute of 4K video at 30 fps consumes roughly 350-400 MB of storage. Record at 60 fps and that jumps to about 600 MB per minute.

HDR and Dolby Vision

Starting with the iPhone 12, iPhones record HDR video in Dolby Vision by default. HDR video encodes a wider range of brightness and color, which requires more data per frame. This is why videos from newer iPhones often look stunning but are even larger than their SDR counterparts.

ProRes and Log Recording

Pro models support Apple ProRes, a professional codec designed for editing flexibility. A single minute of ProRes 4K can consume 6 GB or more. While most users do not shoot ProRes, even standard recordings benefit from compression.

HEVC (H.265) Codec

iPhones default to the HEVC codec, which is already about 40-50% more efficient than the older H.264. However, even with HEVC, high-resolution video is inherently data-heavy. If you have older videos recorded in H.264, they may be even larger than necessary and are prime candidates for recompression.

How Much Space Can Video Compression Save?

The answer depends on the original resolution and the preset you choose. Here are real numbers based on a typical 1-minute video recorded on an iPhone at 30 fps.

Video compression savings by preset
Original Preset Resulting Size Savings
4K 30fps (~400 MB) 720p ~45 MB ~89%
4K 30fps (~400 MB) 1080p ~120 MB ~70%
4K 30fps (~400 MB) 4K half-bitrate ~220 MB ~45%
1080p 30fps (~130 MB) 720p ~50 MB ~62%
1080p 30fps (~130 MB) 1080p half-bitrate ~70 MB ~46%

For a typical library with 50-100 videos, compression can easily reclaim 10-30 GB of storage — often more than enough to avoid paying for iCloud storage upgrades.

Methods to Compress Videos on iPhone

There are several ways to compress videos on an iPhone. Here is how the most popular options compare.

iMovie

Built-in

Apple's free video editor can export projects at lower resolutions. You must create a project, import the video, add it to the timeline, and then export at a reduced resolution.

Pros

  • Pre-installed on every iPhone
  • No download required
  • Apple-quality encoding

Cons

  • One video at a time — no batch processing
  • Must create a project for each video
  • Limited export quality options
  • Slow for large libraries

Shortcuts

Built-in

The Shortcuts app lets you build an automation to encode video at a different size. It works but requires setup and can be fragile with large files.

Pros

  • Free and pre-installed
  • Customizable presets
  • Can process multiple videos with a loop

Cons

  • Requires building or importing a shortcut
  • No progress indication for long batches
  • Can crash on very large files
  • No preview before committing

Every compression preset involves a tradeoff. Here is a clear comparison to help you decide.

When You Should Not Compress Videos

Compression is not always the right move. Here are situations where you should keep the original file.

  • Professional or commercial use. If a video will be edited in Final Cut Pro, color-graded, or used in a production pipeline, always work from the original source file. Re-encoding introduces generational loss.
  • Legal or evidentiary recordings. Videos intended as evidence or for legal purposes should remain in their original, unmodified form to maintain chain of custody.
  • Irreplaceable once-in-a-lifetime moments. A wedding ceremony, a child's first steps, or a graduation may deserve the highest possible quality. Keep these originals and compress copies for everyday viewing.
  • Already heavily compressed footage. If a video has already been compressed (e.g., received via messaging app or downloaded from social media), recompressing it further will degrade quality noticeably with minimal size savings.
  • ProRes or Log footage you plan to grade. The whole point of shooting in ProRes or Log is to preserve maximum dynamic range for post-production. Compressing this defeats the purpose.

When in doubt, compress a copy and keep the original. TidyByte saves compressed videos as new files, so you always have the option to compare before deleting anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does compressing a video on iPhone reduce quality?

Yes, compressing a video does reduce quality to some degree, but the difference is often imperceptible at higher presets. For example, re-encoding a 4K video to 1080p typically cuts file size by 60-70% while looking nearly identical on a phone screen. TidyByte offers multiple presets so you can choose the right balance between size and quality for your needs.

What is the best video format for saving space on iPhone?

HEVC (H.265) is the most space-efficient codec supported natively on iPhone. It typically produces files 40-50% smaller than the older H.264 (AVC) codec at the same visual quality. Modern iPhones already record in HEVC by default. If you have older videos in H.264, recompressing them to HEVC can reclaim significant storage.

How much space can I save by compressing iPhone videos?

Savings depend on the original resolution and your chosen preset. A typical 1-minute 4K/30fps clip is about 400 MB. Compressing to 1080p reduces it to roughly 120 MB (70% savings). Even compressing to 4K at half bitrate saves about 40% while keeping full resolution. Most users can reclaim 10-30 GB of storage by compressing their video library.

Can I compress videos on iPhone without a computer?

Yes. Apps like TidyByte compress videos entirely on your iPhone — no computer, no cloud upload, no account required. All processing happens on-device using Apple's hardware-accelerated video encoder. This means your videos never leave your phone, preserving your privacy.

Is it safe to delete the original video after compressing?

It depends on the video. For casual recordings like family moments or everyday clips, a compressed copy is perfectly fine. However, if the video has professional, legal, or archival value, keep the original. TidyByte always saves the compressed version as a new file so you can compare before deciding whether to delete the original.
