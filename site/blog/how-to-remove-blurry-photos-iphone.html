How to Remove Blurry Photos from Your iPhone

Every iPhone user has them: dozens or hundreds of blurry, dark, and shaky photos that nobody will ever look at again. They waste storage, clutter your camera roll, and make it harder to find the photos that matter. This guide walks you through using TidyByte to delete blurry photos from your iPhone in minutes — safely, and for free.

TidyByte's Blur Detection

TidyByte is a free, open-source iPhone app that scans your entire photo library and identifies low-quality images using Apple's Vision framework. The same machine learning engine that powers Face ID and Live Text also happens to be excellent at measuring image sharpness. TidyByte leverages this framework to perform per-pixel analysis of every photo in your library, calculating edge contrast and high-frequency detail to produce a blur score for each image.

The detection process runs in three phases. First, TidyByte loads a downscaled version of each image to keep memory usage low and processing fast. Second, it applies a Laplacian-style edge detection filter that highlights areas of sharp contrast — the edges between objects, text, textures, and other fine details. Third, it measures how much of the image contains these high-frequency components. A sharp photo has strong edges throughout; a blurry photo has weak, diffuse edges or none at all.

The entire scan happens on your device. No photos are uploaded, no data leaves your phone, and no account is required. For a typical 5,000-photo library, the scan completes in under a minute. The app also checks for exposure problems in the same pass, so you get a single set of results covering blurry, dark, and overexposed photos all at once. If you're looking to delete blurry photos from your iPhone without spending hours swiping through your camera roll one by one, this is the fastest way to do it.

What Gets Flagged as Blurry

Not every slightly soft photo gets flagged. TidyByte's thresholds are tuned to catch images that are genuinely unusable while leaving intentional soft-focus portraits and artistic shots alone. Here is what the detector looks for:

  • Camera shake blur — your hand moved during the exposure, producing a uniformly soft or streaky image. This is the most common type of blurry photo, especially in low light where the shutter stays open longer.
  • Out-of-focus blur — the camera focused on the wrong subject or failed to lock focus entirely. The result is a photo where the main subject is a soft blob and the background may or may not be sharp.
  • Motion blur — the subject moved during the exposure. Unlike camera shake, the blur is directional: a running child appears as a streak while the background stays relatively sharp.
  • Extreme underexposure — the photo is so dark that details are lost in shadow. This commonly happens at concerts, in dimly lit rooms, or when shooting into backlight. TidyByte flags photos where the histogram is heavily weighted toward black.
  • Extreme overexposure — highlights are blown out to pure white, washing out the entire image. This happens with direct flash at close range, shooting toward the sun, or misconfigured exposure compensation.
  • Accidental pocket photos — images captured while the phone was in a pocket or bag. These are typically completely black or a chaotic smear of color with zero useful content.

TidyByte assigns each photo a quality score that combines sharpness and exposure metrics. Photos below a configurable threshold get flagged for review. You can see the breakdown in our guide to finding blurry photos on iPhone.

Reviewing the Results

Once the scan finishes, TidyByte presents every flagged photo in a review interface. Each image is displayed at full resolution so you can see exactly what the detector found. You are in control at every step: swipe right to keep a photo, swipe left to mark it for deletion, or tap to view it full-screen and zoom into details.

The review screen also shows the blur score for each image so you understand why it was flagged. Photos with the lowest quality scores appear first, meaning you see the worst offenders before you get to borderline cases. This ordering saves time because you can quickly confirm the obviously bad ones and spend more time on the ones that are closer to the threshold.

For users who prefer a faster workflow, TidyByte offers two bulk options. Select All marks every flagged photo for deletion in a single tap, which is useful when the detector's accuracy is high and the photos are clearly bad. Select by category lets you bulk-select only blurry photos, only dark photos, or only overexposed photos, which gives you more control over what gets removed. Both approaches are significantly faster than going photo by photo, and both let you deselect individual images before confirming.

Batch Deletion

Batch deletion is where TidyByte saves the most time. Instead of deleting photos one at a time through the Photos app — which requires selecting each image individually, confirming, and then navigating back — TidyByte lets you confirm hundreds of deletions in a single action.

After you finish reviewing and marking photos, tap the delete button to remove all marked images at once. TidyByte uses the PhotoKit API to delete photos from your library, which means the deletion follows the same rules as deleting from the Photos app: every photo moves to the Recently Deleted album, where it stays for 30 days before being permanently removed. During that 30-day window, you can open the Photos app, navigate to Recently Deleted, and recover any photo you want back.

Space Savings

Blurry photos are not just visual clutter — they consume real storage. A single photo taken with an iPhone 14 or later averages 3–5 MB, and Live Photos or images shot in ProRAW can exceed 25 MB each. Even if every blurry photo in your library is a standard JPEG or HEIF, the total adds up quickly.

Based on analysis of typical libraries, here is what you can expect to recover:

Library size Typical blurry photos Estimated storage % of library
2,000 photos 50–100 200–400 MB 2–5%
5,000 photos 100–250 400 MB–1 GB 2–5%
10,000 photos 200–500 1–3 GB 2–5%
20,000+ photos 400–1,000+ 2–5 GB 2–5%

These numbers are conservative. If your library includes a lot of Live Photos, burst shots, or images from events with challenging lighting, the actual number of blurry photos can be higher. Deleting them is one of the easiest ways to free up storage without sacrificing any photos you actually care about. Prevention Tips

Cleaning up blurry photos is reactive. Here is how to take fewer of them in the first place, so you spend less time cleaning and more time enjoying your photos.

  • Tap to focus before shooting. Before pressing the shutter, tap on your subject to lock focus and exposure. This takes a fraction of a second and prevents the camera from focusing on the wrong part of the scene. On iPhone, the yellow square that appears confirms where the camera is focusing.
  • Hold the phone with both hands. Grip the phone with both hands and tuck your elbows into your body. This three-point stabilization dramatically reduces camera shake, especially in low light when the shutter is open longer.
  • Use the volume button as a shutter. Pressing the volume-up button to take a photo causes less hand movement than tapping the screen. It also works with the volume buttons on wired and Bluetooth headphones, giving you a remote shutter option.
  • Keep Live Photos on. Live Photos capture 1.5 seconds of video around each shot. If the key frame is blurry, you can open the photo, tap Edit, and scrub to a sharper frame. This alone can save many photos that would otherwise be deleted.
  • Use Night Mode in low light. On iPhone 11 and later, Night Mode activates automatically when it detects low light. Hold the phone steady (or rest it on a surface) and let the exposure complete. The resulting photo will be significantly sharper and brighter than a standard shot.
  • Clean the lens regularly. A fingerprint smudge on the camera lens causes soft, hazy photos that look out of focus even when the camera locked on correctly. Wipe the lens with a soft cloth before important shots — it takes two seconds and makes a visible difference.
  • Disable the shutter sound and use burst mode for action. When shooting fast-moving subjects, hold down the shutter button to activate burst mode. This captures 10 frames per second, giving you a much better chance that at least one frame is sharp.
  • Run a monthly cleanup. Even with good habits, blurry photos will still accumulate. Open TidyByte once a month for a quick scan. A 60-second review each month prevents the problem from building into a weekend-long cleanup project. See our full guide on organizing your iPhone photos for more maintenance strategies.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remove blurry photos from my iPhone?

Download TidyByte from the App Store, open the Blurry Photos tool, and let the on-device scan complete. The app presents each flagged photo so you can review and delete them individually or in bulk. Deleted photos move to the iOS Recently Deleted album for 30 days before permanent removal, so you always have a safety net.

Can I automatically delete blurry photos?

TidyByte detects blurry photos automatically but always shows them to you before deletion. You can use Select All to remove every flagged photo at once, which is nearly automatic, but the app intentionally asks for your confirmation to prevent accidental deletions of photos you want to keep. This design choice keeps you in control while still saving significant time compared to manual deletion.

What does TidyByte's blur detector look for?

TidyByte analyzes each photo's edge contrast and high-frequency detail using Apple's Vision framework. It measures how many crisp transitions between light and dark exist in the image. Photos with weak or missing sharp transitions are flagged as blurry. It also checks for underexposure and overexposure in the same pass, so you get a single comprehensive report of all low-quality photos in your library.

How many blurry photos does the average person have?

Most iPhone users accumulate between 200 and 500 blurry or poorly-exposed photos over a couple of years. In a typical 10,000-photo library, roughly 2 to 5 percent of images are low-quality enough to delete. These photos can waste 1 to 3 GB of storage, which is enough space to hold thousands of additional high-quality photos or hours of video.

Is it safe to delete blurry photos?

Yes. Photos deleted through TidyByte go to the iOS Recently Deleted album, where they remain for 30 days. You can recover any accidentally deleted photo from that album at any time during the 30-day window. No photo is permanently removed until you empty Recently Deleted or the 30-day period expires. This is the same deletion mechanism used by the built-in Photos app.
