iPhone Storage Full? Here's Exactly How to Free Up Space in 2026

That dreaded "Storage Almost Full" alert always seems to appear at the worst moment — right before a video, a download, or an update. Here's every method to reclaim space, ranked by how much storage you'll actually get back.

What "iPhone Storage Full" actually means

When your iPhone warns that storage is full, it doesn't mean every byte is consumed. iOS reserves a buffer — typically 1–2 GB — for system operations like app updates, caching, and temporary files. The warning triggers when available space drops below this threshold.

Your storage is divided into several categories:

  • Photos & Videos — typically the single largest consumer, often 40–70% of used space.
  • Apps — the app binary plus its cached data. Social media apps can balloon to several gigabytes each.
  • Messages — text is small, but photos, videos, and attachments sent over iMessage add up quickly.
  • System Data — caches, logs, fonts, Siri voices, and other OS-level files. This can range from 5–15 GB.
  • Other — Safari cache, Mail attachments, downloaded files, and miscellaneous data.

Understanding where your space goes is the first step. Targeting the right category saves you from deleting things you care about while ignoring the real hogs.

How to check your storage breakdown

Before you start deleting, take 30 seconds to see exactly what's using your space:

  1. Open Settings → General → iPhone Storage. You'll see a color-coded bar showing how your storage is divided.
  2. Scroll down to see each app ranked by storage usage, from largest to smallest.
  3. Tap any app to see a breakdown of the app size vs. its documents and data.

This screen also shows iOS recommendations, such as offloading unused apps or reviewing large message attachments. We'll cover each of these and more below.

8 concrete methods to free up space

These methods are ordered by typical impact. Start from the top for the biggest wins.

1. Delete duplicate photos (1–15 GB)

Duplicates are the low-hanging fruit. iCloud sync, HDR mode, WhatsApp auto-save, and editing apps all create copies you don't need. Most people have hundreds of duplicates without realizing it.

With TidyByte: Open the app, tap "Duplicates", and let it scan your library. It uses SHA-256 hashing for exact matches and Apple Vision for near-duplicates that look the same but differ slightly. Swipe through each group to keep the best version and delete the rest. Most users recover 1–5 GB from duplicates alone.

Without TidyByte: Go to Photos → Albums → Utilities → Duplicates (iOS 16+). This only catches exact byte-for-byte duplicates, missing the near-duplicates, burst leftovers, and similar shots.

2. Remove blurry and junk photos (1–10 GB)

Everyone has them: accidental pocket shots, blurry selfies, test screenshots, dark photos taken with the lens cap on. They pile up silently over months and years.

With TidyByte: Tap "Blurry photos" in the cleanup toolkit. TidyByte uses Apple's Vision framework to analyze each photo for blur and poor exposure, then presents them in a swipe-to-review interface. Swipe left to delete, right to keep. You'll be surprised how many photos you never need to see again.

Without TidyByte: There's no built-in way to detect blurry photos automatically. You'd need to scroll through your entire library manually — impractical for most people.

3. Compress large videos (2–20 GB)

A single 4K 60fps video uses roughly 400 MB per minute. A handful of vacation clips can easily exceed 10 GB. Most of these videos don't need to be in full resolution.

With TidyByte: Tap "Compress videos" to see all your videos sorted by size. Select the ones you want to shrink, choose a quality level, and TidyByte compresses them on-device using AVAssetExportSession. Typical savings are 40–70% of the original file size with minimal visible quality loss.

Without TidyByte: You can manually re-record at lower resolution, but there's no built-in iOS tool to compress existing videos after the fact.

4. Clear screenshots (500 MB – 3 GB)

Screenshots accumulate from receipts, conversations, maps, recipes, and random things you meant to look at later. Most become irrelevant within days.

With TidyByte: The "Screenshots" tool shows all your screenshots in a review queue. Swipe through and delete the ones you no longer need.

Without TidyByte: Go to Photos → Albums → Media Types → Screenshots and manually select and delete them.

5. Review burst photos (500 MB – 5 GB)

Holding the shutter button captures 10 frames per second. Most people keep one or two shots from a burst and forget to delete the other 8–30 frames. Each frame is a full-resolution photo.

Go to Photos → Albums → Media Types → Bursts to review them. Open each burst, tap "Select", choose the best frame(s), and delete the rest.

6. Convert Live Photos to stills (1–5 GB)

A Live Photo captures 1.5 seconds of video before and after the shutter press, roughly doubling the file size. A typical still is 2–5 MB, while a Live Photo is 5–12 MB.

To convert: open a Live Photo, tap Edit, tap the Live Photo icon (concentric circles) at the top to disable the Live Photo effect, then tap Done. This removes the video component and keeps only the still image. Do this in bulk for older Live Photos you don't need animated.

7. Offload unused apps (1–10 GB)

Apps you haven't opened in months are wasting space. iOS can automatically offload apps you don't use while keeping their data intact.

Go to Settings → App Store → Offload Unused Apps and enable it. Or manually offload individual apps from Settings → General → iPhone Storage by tapping the app and selecting "Offload App". When you reinstall, your data is restored.

8. Clear Safari cache and Messages attachments (500 MB – 3 GB)

Safari stores website data, cached pages, and offline reading list items. Messages stores every photo, video, and file you've ever sent or received.

  • Safari: Go to Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data.
  • Messages: Go to Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Messages. Review and delete large attachments, photos, and videos. You can also set Messages to auto-delete after 30 days or 1 year instead of keeping everything forever.

How to prevent your iPhone from filling up again

Cleaning up once is great. Keeping it clean is better. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Enable "Optimize iPhone Storage" in Settings → Photos. This keeps smaller versions on-device and stores full-resolution photos in iCloud.
  • Turn off "Keep Normal Photo" in Settings → Camera to stop saving both HDR and non-HDR versions of every shot.
  • Disable auto-save in WhatsApp (Settings → Chats → Save to Camera Album). Downloaded media from group chats is a major source of clutter.
  • Set Messages to auto-delete after 1 year instead of forever (Settings → Messages → Message History).
  • Run a monthly cleanup with TidyByte. Open the app, swipe through your latest photos, and delete duplicates and blurry shots before they pile up. A two-minute habit saves hours of cleanup later.
  • Offload unused apps automatically via Settings → App Store → Offload Unused Apps.

Frequently asked questions

What takes up the most storage on an iPhone?

Photos and videos are by far the largest storage consumers on most iPhones. A single 4K video at 60 fps can use 400 MB per minute. After media, the next biggest consumers are apps (especially social media apps with cached data), Messages with attachments, and system data.

Will deleting photos free up space if I use iCloud Photos?

If you have iCloud Photos enabled with "Optimize iPhone Storage" turned on, your iPhone already stores smaller versions of photos. Deleting photos removes them from both your device and iCloud. To free up device space without losing photos, focus on other methods like clearing app caches, offloading unused apps, and compressing videos.

Is it safe to offload unused apps?

Yes. Offloading an app removes the app binary but keeps all its documents and data. When you reinstall the app, your data is restored. You can offload individual apps or enable automatic offloading in Settings → App Store → Offload Unused Apps.

How much space do Live Photos take compared to still photos?

A Live Photo captures 1.5 seconds of video before and after the shot, roughly doubling the file size. A typical still photo is 2–5 MB, while a Live Photo is 5–12 MB. Converting a large collection of Live Photos to stills can recover several gigabytes.

What is the fastest way to free up space on a full iPhone?

The fastest wins come from deleting duplicate and blurry photos (use TidyByte for a guided swipe workflow), compressing large videos, and offloading unused apps. These three actions alone typically recover 5–20 GB for most users. After that, clear Messages attachments and Safari cache for additional space.
