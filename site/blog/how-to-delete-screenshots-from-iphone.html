How to Delete Screenshots from Your iPhone

Screenshots pile up fast. A quick receipt capture here, a meme save there, and suddenly you have thousands of them eating into your iPhone storage. This guide walks through every method to find and delete screenshots, from the built-in Photos app to TidyByte's batch swipe interface, so you can reclaim gigabytes in minutes.

Finding All Your Screenshots

Before you can delete anything, you need to know where screenshots live. iOS automatically sorts screenshots into a dedicated album, so you do not have to scroll through your entire Camera Roll to find them.

Open the Photos app and tap Albums at the bottom of the screen. Scroll down to the Media Types section and tap Screenshots. Every screenshot on your device appears here in chronological order, with the most recent at the bottom. If you use iCloud Photos, this album syncs across all your Apple devices, so deleting a screenshot on your iPhone also removes it from your iPad and Mac.

The Screenshots album is the single most reliable way to find every screenshot. However, it does not tell you how much storage they occupy. To check total storage usage, go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage and look at the Photos entry. Unfortunately, Apple lumps screenshots in with all photos and videos, so the exact screenshot size is not broken out. A safe estimate is that each screenshot takes 200 KB to 1.5 MB depending on your iPhone model and screen resolution.

  • Photos app > Albums > Media Types > Screenshots — the fastest way to view all screenshots on-device.
  • Search bar in Photos — type "screenshot" to surface screenshots via on-device machine learning, including cropped or edited ones.
  • TidyByte Screenshots tool — groups screenshots by date and shows counts at a glance, so you can decide how aggressively to clean.

Manual Deletion Method

The built-in Photos app gives you full control over which screenshots to delete. This method is best when you want to review each screenshot individually or only delete a small batch.

Step-by-step instructions

  • Step 1: Open the Photos app and navigate to Albums > Media Types > Screenshots.
  • Step 2: Tap Select in the top-right corner.
  • Step 3: Tap each screenshot you want to remove. A blue checkmark appears on selected items.
  • Step 4: To select all screenshots at once, tap Select All in the top-left corner after entering selection mode.
  • Step 5: Tap the trash icon in the bottom-right corner and confirm deletion.
  • Step 6: Go to Albums > Recently Deleted, tap Select, then Delete All to permanently remove them and reclaim storage immediately.

The Recently Deleted album holds items for 30 days before auto-deleting. If you skip this step, the storage is not freed until the retention period expires. This is the most common reason people think they have deleted screenshots but see no change in available storage.

One limitation of the manual method is speed. Tapping through hundreds of screenshots is tedious, and the Photos app can lag when selecting large numbers of items. If you have more than a few hundred screenshots to clear, consider using TidyByte's batch deletion instead.

What Counts as a Screenshot

Not every image that looks like a screenshot is classified as one by iOS. Understanding what the system considers a screenshot helps you clean more effectively and avoid accidentally deleting images you want to keep.

Image Type Classified as Screenshot? Notes
Standard screenshot (Side + Volume Up) Yes Appears in Screenshots album immediately.
Full-page Safari screenshot Yes Saved as PDF, but a PNG thumbnail also lands in the album.
Screen recording frame grab No Extracted frames are saved as regular photos, not screenshots.
Saved image from Messages or WhatsApp No These are regular photos even if the original was a screenshot.
Markup / annotated screenshot Yes Editing a screenshot does not change its classification.
Third-party app captures (e.g., scrolling capture) Sometimes Depends on whether the app uses the system screenshot API.

A common surprise is that full-page Safari screenshots generate both a PDF and a PNG. The PNG shows up in the Screenshots album and can be deleted independently. If you use Safari's full-page capture frequently, these can accumulate quickly because each one captures an entire webpage, often several megabytes in size.

iOS uses on-device machine learning to tag screenshots. This means even if you crop or annotate a screenshot heavily, it will still appear in the Screenshots album. The classification metadata is written at capture time and persists through edits.

Preventing Screenshot Clutter

Deleting screenshots reactively is useful, but reducing the number you create in the first place saves even more time. Here are practical habits and settings that cut down on screenshot accumulation.

Use share-and-delete workflow

When you take a screenshot to send to someone, tap the preview thumbnail that appears in the bottom-left corner. Share it directly from the preview, then tap Delete Screenshot when prompted. This one-tap flow prevents the screenshot from ever reaching your Camera Roll in a meaningful way.

Disable screenshot preview thumbnails

If you find the floating thumbnail distracting or accidentally tap it, you can turn it off. Go to Settings > General > Screen Capture and toggle off Show Preview Thumbnails. Screenshots will still save to your Camera Roll, but you will not be tempted to markup or duplicate them from the preview.

Use web clips and bookmarks instead

Many screenshots are taken to save information from websites: recipes, directions, product prices. Replace these habits with Safari's Share > Add to Reading List, or use the Notes app with a URL link. These alternatives use a fraction of the storage a screenshot does and are searchable.

Set a monthly cleanup reminder

Schedule a recurring reminder on the first of each month to open TidyByte and clear out the past month's screenshots. Five minutes a month prevents the problem from compounding. TidyByte's storage tracker shows you exactly how much space you recovered, which is a satisfying motivator to keep the habit going. You can also explore other TidyByte blog posts for more storage-saving strategies.

Screenshots You Should Keep

Deleting every screenshot is not always the goal. Some screenshots serve as important records. Before you bulk-delete, take a moment to identify the ones worth preserving.

  • Two-factor authentication backup codes. If you saved 2FA recovery codes as a screenshot, store them in a password manager instead and then delete the screenshot. A screenshot of backup codes in your Camera Roll is a security risk if your phone is unlocked.
  • Confirmation screens and receipts. Order confirmations, booking references, and warranty screenshots are handy proof of purchase. Move these to a dedicated folder in Notes or Files, then delete the originals.
  • Conversations with sentimental value. A funny text exchange or a meaningful message from a loved one can be worth keeping. Export these to Notes or a cloud storage service so they are preserved even if you delete the screenshot.
  • Medical or legal documents. Prescription details, insurance cards, and similar records should be moved to a secure location like the Health app or an encrypted note, not left as screenshots.
  • Work-related references. Meeting details, Slack messages, or whiteboard photos may be useful short-term. Archive them to a work folder and purge them once the project is complete.

The pattern is simple: if a screenshot contains information you will need again, move it to a better-organized location. If it was a one-time reference that you have already acted on, delete it. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I delete all screenshots on my iPhone?

Open the Photos app, go to Albums, scroll to Media Types, and tap Screenshots. Tap Select, then Select All, and hit the trash icon. Remember to empty the Recently Deleted album afterward to reclaim the storage immediately. For faster bulk deletion, TidyByte's batch-select feature lets you clear hundreds of screenshots in seconds.

Where are my screenshots stored?

Screenshots are saved to your Camera Roll and also appear in the Screenshots album under Media Types in the Photos app. If iCloud Photos is enabled, they sync across all your Apple devices automatically. You can also find them by searching "screenshot" in the Photos app search bar, which uses on-device machine learning to locate them.

Can I mass delete screenshots?

Yes. The Photos app lets you select and delete multiple screenshots at once from the Screenshots album using Select All. For a faster workflow, TidyByte groups screenshots by date and lets you swipe or batch-delete hundreds in seconds. Users report clearing over 500 screenshots in under five minutes with the swipe interface.

How do I stop screenshots from saving to my camera roll?

iOS does not offer a native toggle to disable screenshot saving entirely. The best approach is to delete unwanted screenshots promptly, use the screenshot quick-action preview to share and delete in one tap, or use a cleanup app like TidyByte on a regular basis. You can also turn off the floating preview thumbnail in Settings to reduce accidental captures.

Does TidyByte have a screenshot cleaner?

Yes. TidyByte includes a dedicated Screenshots tool that surfaces every screenshot in your library, groups them by date, and lets you swipe to keep or delete. You can also use Select All to batch-delete an entire group at once. The app is free, open source, and processes everything on-device so your photos never leave your phone.
