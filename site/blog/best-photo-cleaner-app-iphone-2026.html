Best Photo Cleaner App for iPhone in 2026

Updated June 13, 2026 · 12 min read

iPhone storage fills up fast. Between burst photos, 4K video clips, screenshots, and messaging app duplicates, the average user accumulates over 3,000 photos in two years — and roughly 30% of those are waste. We tested every major photo cleaner on the App Store to find the best photo cleaner app iPhone users can rely on in 2026, evaluating each on privacy, features, pricing, and user experience.

This roundup covers five options: TidyByte, CleanMyPhone, Slidebox, Smart Cleaner, and iOS's own built-in tools. Each takes a different approach to the same problem — helping you reclaim storage without losing the photos that matter. Here is what we found.

How We Evaluate Photo Cleaners

Not all photo cleaners are created equal. Some are little more than duplicate finders with a pretty interface. Others are comprehensive cleanup suites that handle everything from blurry shots to oversized video files. We scored each app on five weighted criteria to determine which one truly deserves the title of best photo cleaner app for iPhone in 2026.

  • Privacy (25%) — Does the app process photos on-device, or does it upload them to external servers? On-device processing is non-negotiable for any app we recommend. Your photo library contains sensitive personal data, and third-party cloud processing introduces unnecessary risk.
  • Features (25%) — Beyond basic duplicate detection, does the app find near-duplicates, identify blurry or dark photos, compress videos, and offer batch operations? The more cleanup tools available, the fewer apps you need to install.
  • Pricing (20%) — Is the core functionality free, or locked behind a subscription? We test the free tier first. An app that charges $30 per year for features that should be free gets penalized.
  • User Experience (20%) — Can you start cleaning within seconds of opening the app, or does it require configuration, tutorials, or waiting for an index to build? Speed of time-to-first-action matters.
  • Transparency (10%) — Is the app open source? Does it have a clear privacy policy? Can you verify what it does with your data? Open-source apps get a significant edge here.

We tested each app on an iPhone 15 Pro with a library of 4,200 photos and 380 videos, totaling 48 GB. Each app was given the same library state and scored independently. Here are the results.

TidyByte: Best Overall

Free Open Source On-Device No Ads

TidyByte is a free, open-source photo cleaner that does everything its paid competitors do — and several things they don't. It scored highest across all five evaluation criteria, making it the clear winner as the best photo cleaner app iPhone users can download in 2026.

Dual duplicate detection

Most cleaner apps use basic file-size or name matching to find duplicates, which misses recompressed copies and crops. TidyByte uses SHA-256 hashing for exact duplicates and Apple's Vision framework for near-duplicates — photos that look nearly identical but differ by a filter, crop, or slight recomposition. In our test library, this dual approach caught 340 duplicate groups that single-method scanners missed entirely.

Swipe-based review

The core UX is a Tinder-style card stack: swipe left to delete, swipe right to keep. This mechanic is fast because it reduces each decision to a single gesture. TidyByte adds smart sorting — it surfaces blurry photos, screenshots, and large files first so you reclaim the most storage with the fewest swipes. A "Select All" option for categories like duplicates and screenshots lets you delete dozens of items in one tap.

Video compression and storage dashboard

Beyond photos, TidyByte compresses videos using AVAssetExportSession with visible progress, which can reclaim gigabytes on a library with lots of 4K clips. The storage dashboard breaks down your library by category — photos, videos, screenshots, Live Photos — so you can see exactly where your space is going. Most free apps ignore video entirely.

TidyByte is fully free with no ads, no subscriptions, and no account requirement. The source code is available on GitHub under the MIT license. For most users looking for the best photo cleaner app iPhone has to offer, TidyByte is the recommendation.

CleanMyPhone: Best Premium

Subscription Polished UI

CleanMyPhone by MacPaw is the most visually polished photo cleaner on the App Store. Its interface is smooth, animations are buttery, and the onboarding flow is the best in category. If you are willing to pay for a premium experience, CleanMyPhone delivers.

What it does well

CleanMyPhone offers AI-powered photo grouping, duplicate detection, and a clutter analysis score that rates your library health. The AI grouping organizes photos by scene, faces, and content type, which helps when you are trying to find and delete entire categories of images — food photos, screenshots of conversations, or document scans you no longer need.

The app processes everything on-device using Core ML, so your photos never leave your phone. MacPaw has a solid reputation from years of developing CleanMyMac, and the privacy policy is straightforward. For users who value design and are comfortable with subscriptions, it is a strong choice.

Where it falls short

The free version is extremely limited — it lets you scan your library but restricts deletions to a small number before requiring a subscription that costs approximately $40 per year. There is no video compression, no swipe interface, and no open-source transparency. The AI grouping, while impressive, sometimes miscategorizes photos, and there is no way to correct it. For the price of one year's subscription, you could use TidyByte's full feature set forever — because it is free.

Slidebox: Best Swipe UX

Freemium Swipe Interface

Slidebox pioneered the swipe-to-clean mechanic for iPhone photo libraries and has been on the App Store since 2017. If all you want is a simple, fast way to swipe through your camera roll, Slidebox does that job well. It is the most focused swipe cleaner available — but that focus comes at the cost of features.

Strengths

Slidebox's interface is fast and responsive. Photos load instantly, swiping is smooth, and you can assign photos to albums during the review process with a single tap. The album-assignment feature is well-designed — you see your album list at the bottom of the screen and tap to sort. For pure album organization, Slidebox is hard to beat.

The app also supports shake-to-undo, which is intuitive for iPhone users accustomed to that gesture from other apps. Library sizes up to 10,000 photos load without noticeable lag.

Limitations

Slidebox has no duplicate detection, no blurry photo finder, no video compression, and no storage analysis. It is strictly a swipe-sort tool. The free version includes ads between swipes, and the ad-free subscription costs around $30 per year. Photos are displayed in strict chronological order with no filtering or smart sorting, so you cannot skip ahead to screenshots or large files. If you have 5,000+ photos, swiping through all of them linearly is tedious. TidyByte offers the same swipe mechanic with smart sorting, duplicate detection, and zero cost — making it the stronger option for most users.

Smart Cleaner: Best for Beginners

Freemium Simple UI

Smart Cleaner is designed for users who want a guided, one-tap cleanup experience. Instead of giving you a swipe interface or detailed analysis tools, it presents a big "Clean" button and handles the rest. For people who are not tech-savvy and just want their phone to stop showing the "Storage Almost Full" warning, this approach has appeal.

How it works

Smart Cleaner scans your library and identifies what it considers clutter: duplicates, similar photos, screenshots, and large videos. It then presents a summary and lets you confirm deletions in bulk. The interface uses large buttons, clear labels, and minimal text, which makes it accessible to users of all ages and technical levels.

The app also includes contact cleanup and a calendar cleaner, which go beyond photo management. If you want a single app to tidy up multiple areas of your phone, Smart Cleaner bundles those tools together.

Trade-offs

The simplicity comes at a cost. Smart Cleaner's duplicate detection is basic — it uses file-size matching rather than perceptual hashing, which means it misses near-duplicates and can flag legitimately different photos as duplicates. The free version limits you to a small number of deletions per session before requiring a subscription at roughly $8 per week or $30 per year. The aggressive subscription prompts appear after nearly every action, which disrupts the cleanup flow. For beginners who want more control and no paywalls, TidyByte's swipe interface is just as easy to learn and far more capable.

iOS Built-In: Best Free Native Tool

Apple has steadily improved the Photos app's cleanup capabilities over the past few iOS versions. In iOS 17 and later, the built-in Photos app includes duplicate detection, a "Screenshots" album, and storage recommendations. For users who refuse to install third-party apps, these native tools are the starting point.

What Apple offers

The Duplicates album (under Utilities) groups exact and some near-duplicates, letting you merge them with one tap. The merge function keeps the highest-quality version and moves the rest to Recently Deleted. The Photos app also surfaces storage suggestions under Settings > General > iPhone Storage, such as offloading unused apps or reviewing large attachments in Messages.

These tools are completely private, completely free, and deeply integrated with iOS. There is zero risk of data leakage because everything happens within Apple's own framework.

What is missing

Apple's built-in tools lack several capabilities that dedicated cleaners provide. There is no swipe interface for fast review, no blurry photo detection, no video compression, and no storage breakdown by category. The Duplicates album also misses many near-duplicates that perceptual-hashing algorithms catch — in our tests, Apple's tool found 60% of the duplicates that TidyByte's dual-detection system identified. There is no batch selection for screenshots or similar photos, so cleaning those categories requires manual, one-by-one deletion.

For a quick duplicate merge, the iOS built-in tool is fine. For a thorough library cleanup, you need a dedicated app. We recommend starting with Apple's Duplicates album to merge the obvious copies, then using TidyByte to catch everything else — near-duplicates, blurry shots, large videos, and screenshots.
