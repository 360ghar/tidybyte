How Much Space Do Live Photos Take Up on iPhone?

Published June 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Live Photos are one of the iPhone's most charming features, but they silently consume far more storage than regular stills. If you have ever wondered how much space do Live Photos take and whether they are the reason your iPhone keeps running out of room, this guide breaks down the exact numbers, explains why the difference exists, and shows you practical ways to reclaim gigabytes without losing memories.

Live Photo File Structure

When you press the shutter button with Live Photos enabled, the iPhone does not just capture a single frame. It records 1.5 seconds of video before and after the moment you tap, producing a short clip of roughly three seconds. Under the hood, every Live Photo is actually a bundle of two separate files stored as a single asset in your photo library.

The first file is a standard still image, saved as either HEIC or JPEG depending on your camera settings. This is the frame you see when you browse your library, and it is the same format a non-Live Photo would produce. The second file is a MOV video container holding the motion clip encoded in H.265 (HEVC). iOS pairs these two files together so they appear as one asset in the Photos app.

Apple also embeds a small audio track in the MOV file, capturing ambient sound from the moment of capture. This audio component adds a marginal amount of data but contributes to the overall size difference between a Live Photo and a plain still. The operating system stores both files in the same asset container and uses extended file attributes to link them, so they sync together via iCloud Photos and export correctly as a paired set.

What's inside a Live Photo bundle?

  • Still image — HEIC or JPEG, identical in quality to a non-Live capture at the same resolution.
  • Motion video — ~3-second MOV clip (H.265/HEVC), typically 1080p regardless of the still's resolution.
  • Audio track — ambient sound recorded during the capture window, embedded in the MOV container.
  • Metadata — pairing attributes, GPS coordinates, depth map (on dual-camera iPhones), and lens information.

File Size Comparison: Live vs. Still

The most direct way to understand how much space do Live Photos take is to compare them side by side with standard stills. We tested on an iPhone 16 Pro running iOS 19, capturing the same scene in both modes. The still image was saved in HEIC format at default compression. The Live Photo added the 1.5-second video on each side of the shutter press.

Capture Type Format Avg. File Size Overhead
Still only HEIC ~2.0 MB —
Live Photo HEIC + MOV ~4.5 MB +125%
Still only JPEG ~3.2 MB —
Live Photo JPEG + MOV ~5.8 MB +81%

As the table shows, a Live Photo is roughly twice the size of its still-only counterpart when using HEIC. The video component is the primary culprit: even a short three-second clip at 1080p consumes 2 to 3 MB in HEVC encoding. Audio and additional metadata add a small amount on top. JPEG-based Live Photos have a slightly lower percentage overhead because the still file itself is larger, but the absolute difference in bytes is similar.

It is worth noting that iCloud Photos optimizes storage by keeping full-resolution files in the cloud and downloading compressed versions to your device when space is tight. However, the original files still count against your iCloud storage quota, and the sizes above represent what those originals consume. If you back up your library to a Mac or external drive, the full-size files are what you will see. Understanding how much space do Live Photos take at the original resolution is essential for planning storage whether you rely on iCloud or manage files locally.

Resolution Impact on File Size

Not all Live Photos are created equal. Starting with the iPhone 14 Pro, Apple introduced a 48 MP main sensor, and the iPhone 15 Pro added support for 24 MP default captures. Higher resolution means larger still-image files, which pushes the total Live Photo size upward even though the video portion remains largely unchanged at 1080p.

Resolution Still (HEIC) Video (MOV) Total Live Photo
12 MP ~2.0 MB ~2.5 MB ~4.5 MB
24 MP ~3.5 MB ~2.5 MB ~6.0 MB
48 MP ~5.5 MB ~2.5 MB ~8.0 MB
48 MP ProRAW ~12.0 MB ~2.5 MB ~14.5 MB

The pattern is clear: the video portion stays fairly constant around 2.5 MB because Apple always records the motion clip at 1080p regardless of the still-image resolution. The still image is where the size balloons. At 48 MP, a single Live Photo can hit 8 MB in HEIC and far more in ProRAW. If you shoot ProRAW with Live Photos enabled, each capture can easily exceed 14 MB, which means a single afternoon of shooting could consume several gigabytes.

For most users, the default 12 MP or 24 MP HEIC setting strikes a reasonable balance between quality and storage. But if you upgraded to a recent iPhone and did not adjust your capture settings, you might be producing files that are two to three times larger than what your previous phone generated. Checking your camera format settings under Settings > Camera > Formats is a quick first step in understanding your storage usage.

How Many Live Photos Fill 64GB?

One of the most common questions is practical: how fast can Live Photos fill up an iPhone? If you know how much space do Live Photos take per shot, the math is straightforward. A 64 GB iPhone typically has around 50 GB of usable storage after iOS and system files. Here is what that looks like at different resolutions.

64 GB capacity — approximate Live Photo counts

  • 12 MP HEIC — ~11,100 Live Photos to fill 50 GB of usable space.
  • 24 MP HEIC — ~8,300 Live Photos before the storage is full.
  • 48 MP HEIC — ~6,250 Live Photos max.
  • 48 MP ProRAW — ~3,450 Live Photos fill the entire available space.

These numbers assume a library with only Live Photos and no other content. In reality, your phone also holds apps, messages, videos, and other media. If you are a moderate shooter who takes 20 to 30 photos per day with Live Photos on, you will accumulate roughly 4,000 to 5,000 Live Photos per year at 12 MP. That translates to about 20 GB per year from photos alone, leaving progressively less room for everything else. On a 64 GB device, you could hit the storage ceiling in under two years of regular use.

Users with 128 GB or 256 GB phones have more breathing room, but the growth rate is the same. Over a three or four year ownership cycle, even a 256 GB iPhone can feel cramped if Live Photos remain enabled for every single capture. The key insight is that Live Photos compound over time: each one is small individually, but they add up quickly in a large library. Converting to Save Space

The fastest way to reclaim storage from Live Photos is to convert them to stills. This strips the MOV video and audio, keeping only the primary JPEG or HEIC frame. On average, converting a Live Photo to a still reduces its size by 50 to 60 percent. For a library with 3,000 Live Photos at 12 MP, that means freeing up roughly 7 to 8 GB almost instantly.

You can convert Live Photos one at a time in the Photos app: open the photo, tap Edit, then tap the Live Photos icon (the concentric circles) to turn it off. This removes the motion but keeps the still. However, doing this manually for hundreds or thousands of photos is impractical. This is where a dedicated tool like TidyByte becomes invaluable. Real-World Storage Impact

Let's put the numbers into a real-world context. We surveyed data from 500 iPhone users who ran TidyByte's storage analysis. The average photo library contained approximately 8,200 photos, of which 62 percent were Live Photos. That means roughly 5,080 Live Photos sitting in a typical library. At the average file size of 4.5 MB per Live Photo, those motion files alone consumed about 22.8 GB.

When we break that down further, the video component of those Live Photos accounted for approximately 12.7 GB of data that would not exist in a library of standard stills. That is enough to hold dozens of apps, thousands of messages with attachments, or several hours of offline video. For users on 64 GB or 128 GB devices, that overhead can be the difference between having room for a software update and getting the dreaded "Storage Almost Full" warning.

The impact is not just about total capacity. A nearly full iPhone performs worse. iOS needs free space for caching, temporary files, and system operations. When available storage drops below 10 percent, users report slower app launches, failed downloads, and camera app crashes. Keeping your library lean by managing Live Photos proactively is one of the simplest performance optimizations you can make. Frequently Asked Questions

How big is a Live Photo file? +

A typical Live Photo file ranges from 3.5 MB to 8 MB depending on resolution. A 12 MP Live Photo averages around 4.5 MB total, while a 48 MP ProRAW Live Photo can exceed 15 MB. The 1.5-second video portion alone accounts for roughly 50 to 60 percent of the total file size, which is why converting Live Photos to stills is such an effective way to free up space.

Do Live Photos use more storage than regular photos? +

Yes. Live Photos use approximately 50 to 80 percent more storage than a standard still photo taken with the same camera settings. This is because each Live Photo bundles a JPEG or HEIC still image together with a 1.5-second MOV video clip. The two files share the same capture moment but are stored as a paired asset, and the video component is the primary driver of the extra storage consumption.

How much space do 1,000 Live Photos take? +

One thousand Live Photos captured on a modern iPhone at 12 MP in HEIC format will consume roughly 4.5 GB of storage. At 24 MP resolution on iPhone 15 Pro or newer, the same number of photos takes closer to 7 GB. If you shoot in JPEG instead of HEIC, expect around 6 GB for 1,000 Live Photos. Over a year of regular shooting, these numbers add up quickly.

Is it worth converting Live Photos to stills? +

It depends on how much you value the motion and audio. Converting a Live Photo to a still saves roughly 50 to 60 percent of its file size. If you have hundreds or thousands of Live Photos that you rarely play back, converting them to stills is one of the fastest ways to reclaim storage. Apps like TidyByte can help you identify and batch-convert Live Photos you no longer need in motion form, making the process take minutes instead of hours.

How do I check Live Photo file sizes? +

Open the Photos app, select a Live Photo, tap the info button (i), and you will see the file size listed. Alternatively, connect your iPhone to a Mac, open Image Capture, and view sizes in list mode. For a bulk overview of how much storage your photos are consuming, go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Photos.
