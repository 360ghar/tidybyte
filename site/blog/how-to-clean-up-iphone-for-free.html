Photo Library Cleanup

Photos and videos are almost always the single largest consumer of iPhone storage. A typical library with 3,000 photos and a few dozen videos can easily occupy 30 to 60 GB. According to Apple, the Camera app shoots 12 MP HEIF photos at roughly 1-3 MB each, but 4K video at 60 fps consumes about 400 MB per minute. Over time, those numbers add up fast.

The first step is to identify what is actually wasting space. Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage and look at the Photos entry. You will likely see it at the top of the list. From there, the cleanup process breaks into three phases:

  • Remove duplicates. Studies show that 10-15% of a typical photo library consists of exact duplicates — burst shots that were never cleaned up, screenshots saved twice, or photos re-downloaded from messages. A duplicate photo finder can detect these automatically using file hashing, saving you from comparing them side by side.
  • Delete blurry and low-quality shots. Every library has them: out-of-focus selfies, motion-blurred pet photos, and accidental pocket shots. These are safe to delete and often account for another 5-10% of your library.
  • Clear screenshots and screen recordings. Screenshots pile up quickly, especially if you use them for temporary reference. Most people have hundreds they have forgotten about. Cleaning up screenshots alone can recover several gigabytes.

TidyByte makes this entire process fast and free. It is a free, open-source iPhone photo cleaner that uses a swipe-based interface — swipe right to keep, swipe left to delete. You can review 400-600 photos in a 15-minute session. Its built-in duplicate detection finds exact and near-duplicates automatically, and it can compress large videos to save additional space. Everything runs 100% on your device with no account required.

App Offloading and Deletion

After photos, apps are the second biggest storage consumer. Games can occupy 1-5 GB each, streaming apps cache large amounts of media, and social media apps like Instagram and TikTok store hundreds of megabytes in local data. A 128 GB iPhone can easily have 30-40 GB tied up in apps, many of which you have not opened in months.

iOS provides two built-in options under Settings > General > iPhone Storage:

  • Offload App. This removes the app binary but preserves its documents and data. The app icon stays on your home screen with a cloud download symbol. When you need it again, iOS re-downloads it and your data is still there. This is ideal for apps you use occasionally, like travel or event apps.
  • Delete App. This removes the app and all its data completely. Use this for apps you no longer need at all.

iOS also offers automatic offloading: go to Settings > App Store > Offload Unused Apps and enable it. The system will automatically offload apps you have not used in 30 days or more. A quick audit of your storage screen will reveal apps consuming surprising amounts of space — sorting by size and working from the top down is the most efficient approach. In most cases, offloading five to ten unused apps recovers 5-15 GB immediately.

Clearing App Caches

Many apps store temporary data — cached images, downloaded files, preview thumbnails — that grows over time without your awareness. Safari, social media apps, streaming services, and messaging apps are the biggest culprits. Unlike the app binary itself, this cached data does not appear clearly in the iPhone Storage breakdown, which is why it often goes unnoticed.

Here is how to clear caches for the most common offenders:

  • Safari. Go to Settings > Safari > Clear History and Website Data. This removes cached pages, cookies, and browsing history. It can free up hundreds of megabytes to several gigabytes if you have never cleared it.
  • Social media apps. Apps like Instagram, TikTok, and X (Twitter) cache viewed videos and images. The most reliable way to clear this data is to delete and reinstall the app. Your account data is stored server-side, so you will not lose anything important.
  • Streaming apps. Spotify, Netflix, YouTube, and Apple Music store downloaded content and playback caches. Open each app and check its download or offline section to remove saved media you no longer need.

One common misconception is that "clearing RAM" or force-closing apps frees up storage. It does not. Force-closing apps only frees temporary memory (RAM), not storage space. The real storage savings come from clearing cached data as described above. If you want a comprehensive view of what is using space, the iPhone Storage screen in Settings shows each app ranked by total size, including its cache.

Managing Messages Storage

iMessage and SMS conversations accumulate attachments over time — photos, videos, voice memos, GIFs, and documents sent back and forth. A single active group chat can easily consume 5-10 GB if it includes regular photo and video sharing. Apple's own support documentation notes that Messages is often one of the top five storage consumers on a typical iPhone.

iOS gives you a dedicated tool for this. Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Messages. You will see a breakdown of conversations by size, along with categories like Photos, Videos, and Other. From here you can:

  • Review large attachments. Tap "Review Large Attachments" to see every photo and video stored in Messages, sorted by size. Delete the ones you do not need to keep.
  • Set auto-deletion. Go to Settings > Messages > Message History and change "Keep Messages" from "Forever" to "1 Year" or "30 Days." This automatically removes old messages and their attachments, preventing future buildup.
  • Delete entire conversations. For old group chats or conversations you no longer need, swipe left in the Messages app and delete the thread entirely. This removes all associated media at once.

If you want to keep the messages themselves but free up the media space, you can save important photos to your photo library first, then delete the attachments from Messages. Just be aware that those saved photos will now take up space in your photo library, where tools like TidyByte can help you manage them later.

Safari Cache and Data

Safari stores website data, cached pages, cookies, and browsing history to speed up your browsing experience. While this is helpful for frequently visited sites, months or years of accumulated data can consume 500 MB to 3 GB of storage. If you have never cleared your Safari data, this section alone could recover a meaningful amount of space.

To clear Safari data, go to Settings > Safari and tap Clear History and Website Data. This removes your browsing history, cookies, and cached files across all devices signed into the same iCloud account. You will be logged out of websites, so have your passwords ready or make sure they are saved in iCloud Keychain.

For more granular control, go to Settings > Safari > Advanced > Website Data. Here you can see exactly how much data each website has stored and delete it individually. This is useful if you want to keep your login sessions for specific sites while clearing everything else. You can also swipe left on individual entries to delete them one at a time.

If you use Safari's Reading List feature, downloaded articles also take up storage. To clear them, go to Settings > Safari and toggle off "Automatically Save Offline" under Reading List, then manually remove saved articles you have already read. Additionally, if you use Safari's download feature, check the Downloads folder in the Files app and remove any files you no longer need.

Complete Cleanup Checklist

Use this checklist to systematically clean up iPhone storage from top to bottom. Each step targets a different category of waste, and the estimated recovery ranges are based on typical usage patterns. Your actual results will depend on how long it has been since you last cleaned up and how you use your device.

iPhone Storage Cleanup Checklist

Step Action Est. Recovery
1 Delete duplicate and blurry photos with TidyByte 5 - 20 GB
2 Compress large videos in your library 2 - 10 GB
3 Clear the Recently Deleted album in Photos 1 - 5 GB
4 Offload or delete unused apps 3 - 15 GB

After completing the checklist, restart your iPhone. iOS stores temporary files that get cleared on reboot, which can recover an additional 500 MB to 1 GB. Then revisit Settings > General > iPhone Storage to see your new available space. Most users who go through all eight steps recover between 15 and 40 GB — more than enough to keep using their current phone comfortably without upgrading storage or paying for iCloud.

Every method in this guide is free, and the biggest wins — photo cleanup, app offloading, and cache clearing — can be done in under 30 minutes.
