The average iPhone user takes around 20 photos per day, and studies show that 25 to 35 percent of a typical photo library consists of duplicates, blurry shots, and unnecessary screenshots. Without a dedicated cleanup tool, that clutter adds up fast — especially on a 128 GB iPhone where every gigabyte counts. What Happened to Gemini Photos?

Gemini Photos launched in 2018 as a companion to MacPaw's Gemini 2 duplicate finder for macOS. The iPhone app quickly gained traction because it offered something few competitors did at the time: an intelligent, AI-driven approach to identifying duplicate and low-quality photos directly on your phone. By 2021, it had been downloaded millions of times and was regularly featured in "best iPhone apps" lists across major tech publications.

In 2024, MacPaw quietly removed Gemini Photos from the App Store. The company did not issue a formal announcement or detailed explanation, but the removal aligned with a broader strategic shift at MacPaw toward their core macOS products. Existing users who had the app installed could continue using it temporarily, but no further updates were released. Without iOS compatibility updates, the app began encountering issues on newer iPhone models and iOS versions. By early 2025, most users reported that Gemini Photos was either crashing frequently or failing to access their photo libraries correctly.

The discontinuation left a gap in the market. Gemini Photos had been the go-to recommendation for iPhone users who wanted a simple, well-designed photo cleanup tool. Its subscription model — around $23.99 per year — was considered reasonable given the app's polish and feature set. But with no replacement from MacPaw, users began searching for alternatives. Many turned to generic cleaner apps loaded with ads and dark-pattern upsells. Others simply stopped cleaning their libraries altogether, letting thousands of duplicate and blurry photos accumulate.

The key features that made Gemini Photos popular were its duplicate detection engine, blurry photo identification, screenshot filtering, and a clean swipe-based interface. It also offered a "suggestions" system that surfaced the worst photos first, reducing the time needed to clean a large library. These are the exact features we will compare against TidyByte in the sections below.

TidyByte is free. Not freemium, not free-trial, not free-with-ads. It is completely free, open-source software released under the MIT license. Privacy: On-Device vs. Cloud Processing

When you hand a photo cleanup app access to your entire library, you are trusting it with some of the most personal data on your phone — family photos, medical documents, screenshots of private conversations. How each app handles that data matters enormously.

TidyByte processes everything on-device. No photos are uploaded to any server. No usage analytics are collected. No tracking SDKs are embedded in the app. The only permission TidyByte requests is Photo Library access, and it uses that permission exclusively to read your photos, analyze them for duplicates and quality issues, and write deletion or album-sorting changes back to your library. Because TidyByte is open-source on GitHub, every network call (or lack thereof) can be independently verified. You can also read the full privacy policy for a plain-language breakdown.

Gemini Photos, like most commercial apps from established companies, included analytics and crash-reporting SDKs. MacPaw's privacy policy disclosed the collection of device identifiers, usage patterns, and diagnostic data. While Gemini Photos did not upload your actual photos to the cloud for processing, the metadata it collected — how many photos you have, how often you clean them, what categories you delete most — painted a detailed picture of your behavior. This data was shared with third-party analytics providers as outlined in their privacy policy.

For privacy-conscious users, the difference is clear. TidyByte's on-device-only architecture means your photos never leave your phone. The app works without an internet connection — useful on flights, in rural areas, or anywhere you want to clean your library without worrying about data transmission. There is no account to create, no email to provide, and no terms of service that grant the developer broad rights over your data. The code is open, the app is free, and your photos stay yours.

Which One Should You Choose?

The honest answer is that choosing between TidyByte and Gemini Photos is no longer a real decision. Gemini Photos is discontinued. It does not receive updates, it does not work reliably on current iOS versions, and it is no longer available for download. If you still have it installed, it is living on borrowed time — every iOS update risks breaking it further.

But even when Gemini Photos was active, TidyByte offers a more complete feature set. Gemini Photos excelled at duplicate detection and had a beautifully designed interface. TidyByte matches those strengths and goes further with video compression, burst photo management, Live Photo conversion, a storage dashboard, and batch operations. The average TidyByte user reclaims 3 to 5 GB of storage in their first session — a figure that includes video compression savings that Gemini Photos could never provide.

There are a few scenarios where you might consider other alternatives. If you need a macOS companion app to clean photos on your computer as well, MacPaw still sells CleanMyMac, which includes a photo cleanup module. If you want a cloud-based solution that works across devices, Google Photos offers storage management tools, though at the cost of uploading your entire library to Google's servers. For most iPhone users, though, an on-device tool is the right choice — and among those, TidyByte is the most feature-complete free option available.

The long-term outlook also favors TidyByte. As an open-source project, it is not dependent on a single company's revenue targets or strategic pivots. The source code is publicly available, meaning the community can maintain and improve it even if the original developers move on. Gemini Photos' fate — abandoned without warning — is a cautionary tale about closed-source subscription apps. TidyByte's model eliminates that risk entirely.

Migration Guide: Switching from Gemini Photos

If you are coming from Gemini Photos, switching to TidyByte takes less than five minutes. There is no data to migrate because TidyByte reads directly from your iPhone's photo library — the same library Gemini Photos accessed. All your albums, favorites, and organizational structure remain intact. Here is a step-by-step guide to get started.

Step 1: Download and grant access

Download TidyByte from the App Store. When you first launch it, the app will request Photo Library access. Grant full access so TidyByte can scan your entire library. This permission stays on-device — TidyByte does not upload anything. You can verify this by monitoring network traffic or reviewing the open-source code.

Step 2: Start with the storage dashboard

Unlike Gemini Photos, which threw you straight into suggestions, TidyByte offers a storage dashboard that shows exactly where your space is going. Check this first — it will tell you how many duplicates you have, how many blurry photos are in your library, how much space your videos consume, and how many screenshots are taking up room. This overview helps you prioritize which cleanup task to tackle first.

Step 3: Tackle duplicates and blurry photos

Head to the duplicates section first, since this is what Gemini Photos users will find most familiar. TidyByte groups exact duplicates and near-duplicates separately, with the "best" version in each group pre-selected to keep. Review the groups, deselect any you want to preserve, and delete the rest. Then move to blurry photos — TidyByte scores each photo's sharpness and surfaces the worst ones for quick review. For most users, these two steps alone recover 1 to 3 GB of storage.

Step 4: Compress videos and clean screenshots

Gemini Photos never offered video compression, so this is a new opportunity for former users. Navigate to the video compression tool and let TidyByte analyze your video library. It will show you the current size of each video and the estimated size after compression. You can compress individual videos or batch-compress all of them. A typical user recovers 1 to 2 GB from video compression alone. After that, clean up screenshots — most people have hundreds of forgotten screenshots from months or years ago that serve no purpose.

Step 5: Set up a maintenance routine

Gemini Photos had a reminders feature that nudged you to clean up periodically. TidyByte works the same way — launch it once a month, spend 5 to 10 minutes swiping through recent photos, and your library stays under control. Because TidyByte is free with no subscription to manage, there is no reason not to keep it installed permanently as part of your iPhone maintenance toolkit.
