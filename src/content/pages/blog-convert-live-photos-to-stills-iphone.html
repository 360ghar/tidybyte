How to Convert Live Photos to Still Images on iPhone (Save Space)

Live Photos are great for capturing motion and sound, but they consume two to three times more storage than regular photos. If you have hundreds — or thousands — of Live Photos sitting in your library, converting them to still images is one of the fastest ways to reclaim gigabytes of space. This guide walks you through the built-in iPhone method, shows you how to batch convert Live Photos to stills on iPhone with TidyByte, and helps you decide which photos to keep as Live and which to flatten.

Why Convert Live Photos?

Apple introduced Live Photos with the iPhone 6s in 2015. Each Live Photo captures 1.5 seconds of video and audio before and after the shutter press, bundling it with the high-resolution still image. The result is a file that feels more like a tiny video clip than a photo. While this is charming for birthday candles and crashing waves, most Live Photos in a typical library are mundane — a slightly blurry frame of a lunch plate, a barely perceptible head nod, or a desk with faint background noise.

The storage cost adds up quickly. A standard 12 MP JPEG photo averages around 2-3 MB. A Live Photo of the same scene typically runs 5-8 MB because it stores the still image plus a 1080p video track and an AAC audio track. On a 128 GB iPhone where photos already dominate storage, that difference matters. Apple's own storage breakdown tool consistently shows the Photos app as the largest consumer of space on most iPhones.

Converting Live Photos to stills is also useful for sharing. Not every platform preserves the Live Photo animation — email attachments, messaging apps outside iMessage, and social media uploads often strip the video track anyway. Sending a clean still image is more predictable and universally compatible. If you want to optimize your photo library further, converting Live Photos is a high-impact first step.

Built-in Method: One at a Time

Apple provides a straightforward way to convert a single Live Photo to a still image using the built-in Photos app. Open the Live Photo, tap Edit in the top-right corner, then tap the Live Photo icon (concentric circles) at the top of the screen. This disables the Live effect. Tap Done to save. The video and audio tracks are removed, and only the best-quality key frame remains as a standard still photo.

There is an important nuance: Apple does not permanently delete the video data when you toggle off Live in Edit mode. The original is preserved in iCloud Photos as a recoverable version. To permanently free the space, you need to delete the photo and then remove it from the Recently Deleted album. Alternatively, you can use the Duplicate workflow — duplicate the photo as a still, confirm the still looks correct, then delete the original Live Photo entirely.

The limitation is obvious: this is a one-photo-at-a-time process. If you have 300 Live Photos, you are looking at potentially an hour of tapping through Edit screens. Apple does not offer a batch toggle for Live Photos in the Photos app, nor does Shortcuts provide a native "disable Live Photo" action that works reliably at scale. For users with large libraries, a dedicated tool is far more practical.

Quality Comparison: Live vs. Still

A common concern when you convert Live Photos to stills on iPhone is whether the image quality degrades. The short answer: it does not. The still portion of a Live Photo is captured at the same resolution and bit depth as a regular photo. When you extract the key frame, you get the exact same pixel data that the camera sensor recorded at the moment of the shutter press.

The video track in a Live Photo is lower resolution than the still — it is typically 1080p at 15 fps, not the full 12 MP or 48 MP of the still image. So when you convert, you are not downgrading a high-res video to a low-res photo. You are removing a lower-quality video track and keeping the highest-quality component. The resulting still image is identical in sharpness, color accuracy, and dynamic range to the original.

One edge case worth noting: if you took a Live Photo in low light, the iPhone may have used computational photography techniques like Deep Fusion or Night Mode that blend multiple frames. The key frame TidyByte extracts is the final processed image — the same one Apple's Photos app displays when you view the Live Photo as a still. You lose nothing in terms of image processing. The only thing you lose is the 1.5-second animation and the ambient audio, which for most photos is negligible.

When NOT to Convert

Converting Live Photos to stills is not always the right move. Some Live Photos genuinely benefit from the motion and audio. First moments — a child's first steps, a pet's reaction, a surprise proposal — are worth preserving as Live Photos because the motion tells a story that a still cannot. Similarly, nature and travel shots with moving water, wind in trees, or bustling street scenes often feel more vivid as Live Photos.

There is also a creative consideration. Live Photos can be converted to long exposure shots in the Photos app — a waterfall rendered as silky smooth water, or car headlights drawn as light trails. If you have Live Photos that might benefit from this effect, keep them as Live Photos. Once you convert to a still, the long exposure option is gone. Apple's Photos app also supports converting Live Photos to loops and bounces for creative sharing, which requires the video track.

A practical rule of thumb: convert the bulk of your Live Photos (especially the ones you never actually play as Live), but curate a small collection of the ones that genuinely benefit from motion. TidyByte's swipe interface makes this easy — you can quickly scan each Live Photo, decide if the motion adds value, and only convert the ones where the still image tells the full story. For the rest, keep the Live effect and enjoy the extra dimension they bring to your memories.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a Live Photo to a still image?

Open the Live Photo in the Photos app, tap Edit, then tap the Live Photo icon (concentric circles) at the top to disable the Live effect. Tap Done to save. The photo is now stored as a standard still image, and the video portion is removed from storage. This is the built-in way to convert Live Photos to stills on iPhone without any third-party app.

Can I convert multiple Live Photos at once?

Yes. Apple's Photos app does not support batch conversion, but TidyByte can identify all your Live Photos and convert them to stills in a single batch operation. Select the ones you want to keep as stills, and TidyByte handles the rest — no need to open each photo individually.

Does converting Live Photos reduce quality?

No. When you convert a Live Photo to a still, the app extracts the best-quality key frame — the exact same resolution and detail as the original still portion of the Live Photo. You lose the 1.5-second video clip and audio, but the image quality remains identical. There is no recompression or quality loss on the photo itself.

How much space does converting Live Photos save?

A typical Live Photo occupies 3-8 MB because it bundles a 12 MP still image with a 1.5-second 1080p video clip. Converting it to a still typically reduces the file to 1-3 MB — a 40-70% reduction. For a library with 500 Live Photos, you could reclaim 1-2 GB of storage by converting them all to stills.

Is there a free app to batch convert Live Photos?

TidyByte is a free, open-source iOS app that can batch convert Live Photos to still images. It runs entirely on-device with no account required, and processes your photos without uploading anything to the cloud. It also offers other cleanup tools like duplicate detection and screenshot management.
