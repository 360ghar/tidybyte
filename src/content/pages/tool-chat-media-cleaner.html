The album that refills itself

Messaging apps save what you receive into your photo library. WhatsApp creates a WhatsApp album, Telegram creates its own, and the same is true for Signal, Messenger, Viber, LINE and WeChat. It happens quietly, it happens for group chats as much as for friends, and it is the reason a library grows even when you have not taken a photo in weeks.

The contents are predictable: memes, forwarded videos, screenshots of conversations, delivery confirmations, and photos of things people wanted you to see once. Very little of it is worth its storage, and none of it is worth the backup bandwidth it consumes.

Chat media is the clearest example of a pile where the decision is easy and the volume is high, which makes it one of the fastest wins in any cleanup session.

How TidyByte finds chat media

Messengers save into ordinary albums, so the albums themselves are the signal. TidyByte recognises the albums created by WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Telegram, Signal, Messenger, Viber, LINE and WeChat, and pre-selects them. You can also add any other album, which covers apps with unusual names and media saved by hand into a folder of your own.

  1. Confirm which albums count as chat media. The selection is remembered, so you set it once.
  2. Review the list with sizes and dates, sorted by size or by date.
  3. Export anything you want to keep before deleting: a shared video, a photo of a document, a picture of someone you love.
  4. Delete the rest in one pass. One iOS confirmation, 30 days in Recently Deleted.

What is deleted, exactly

Only the copy in your photo library. The chat app keeps its own copy in its private storage, so conversations are unaffected. To clear that side too, use the storage settings inside the chat app itself.

Clear the backlog, then stop the refill

Cleaning is only half the fix. Chat apps keep saving by default, so the album will be full again in a few months unless you change the setting in each app. It takes a minute per messenger and it is the difference between a one-off cleanup and a permanent change.

  • Turn off auto-save in each messenger's settings, so new media stays in the chat instead of the library.
  • Clear the chat app's own storage periodically, which is where the copy inside the app lives.
  • Run a chat media review every few months, since forwards still arrive and you may choose to save some deliberately.
  • Watch the duplicates too: the same image often arrives from two people and lands in your library twice. The duplicate cleaner handles that.

Between the setting and a quarterly pass, chat media stops being a silent storage leak and becomes a category you control.
