The archaeology of a screenshot pile

A screenshot is the shortest-lived kind of photo. You take it to remember a confirmation number, a recipe, a route, a conversation, a QR code that expires in ten minutes. Then it stays in your library forever, mixed in with the photos that actually matter, and every future cleanup has to walk past it again.

Screen recordings are the same problem with a much bigger footprint: a single two-minute recording can be 50 to 200 MB, and nobody ever watches it twice. Between the two, screenshots and recordings are usually the second-largest reclaimable category after video, and the easiest to decide about because almost none of them are sentimental.

How screenshots are detected

iOS labels screenshots in the photo library metadata itself: they carry a screenshot media subtype, and screen recordings carry the video equivalent. TidyByte reads those labels directly, which is why the list it builds is exact rather than a guess based on image dimensions or file names.

That accuracy cuts both ways, and it is worth being straight about the limit: an image saved from a website or an app is an ordinary photo, not a screenshot, so no tool can detect it by subtype. TidyByte’s Smart Categories groups photos that look like they came from apps or the web, which is the honest way to review that pile instead of pretending it can be filtered exactly.

What the list gives you

  • Every screenshot and screen recording, with size and date
  • Sorting by size, so the recordings surface first
  • A running total of the space your selection will return
  • Select all, or keep the handful you still need

A two-minute routine for clearing screenshots

  1. Sort by size and clear the recordings. This is where the megabytes are, and recordings are almost never worth keeping.
  2. Filter by year. Anything older than a year is very unlikely to still be useful, and this is where the volume is.
  3. Scan the recent list for keeps. Receipts, tickets and reference images are usually within the last few weeks; deselect them.
  4. Delete the rest in one pass. One confirmation, and the space is back.

Repeat monthly and the pile never grows large enough to be a project again. The optional weekly reminder is designed for exactly this: a nudge when the reclaimable total creeps up, not a demand to clean everything.

Which screenshots are actually worth keeping

Most screenshots have a shelf life measured in minutes. The exceptions are usually records you would need to produce later, and they are worth protecting before you bulk-delete.

Worth keeping

Receipts and invoices, insurance and warranty details, medical information, travel bookings still ahead of you, recipes you actually cook, and reference images for ongoing projects.

Safe to clear

Expired tickets and QR codes, delivery notifications, error messages you already fixed, memes, and anything you screenshotted to send to someone in a conversation.

If a screenshot turns out to matter after all, it is still in Recently Deleted for 30 days, and restoring it is two taps in the Photos app.
