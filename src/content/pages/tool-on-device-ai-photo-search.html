Why searching beats scrolling

Search is the only feature that gets faster as your library gets bigger. Scrolling gets slower, albums need maintenance, and sorting by year only helps if you remember when something happened. Typing “dogs on a beach” and getting the photos back takes a second, at any library size.

The catch is where that search runs. Cloud photo services answer questions like this by holding your library on their servers, which is why they need an account and a connection. TidyByte does it locally: the content labels come from Apple’s Vision framework on your device, and the interpretation of your sentence happens on the device too.

The practical result is search that works on a plane, with no account, no upload, and no terms of service about what may be done with your photos.

How on-device AI search works

  1. 1. Photos are labelled locally

    During a scan, each photo is classified with Apple Vision into content labels: dog, beach, food, receipt, document, and so on. This happens on your iPhone and produces the vocabulary the search works against.

  2. 2. Your words become a query

    On iPhones where Apple’s on-device language model is available, that model reads your description and turns it into a structured query over those labels. Where it is not available, TidyByte matches your keywords against the same labels directly, and says which method it used.

  3. 3. Matching happens in the app

    The query is validated against the labels that actually exist in your scan, then matched in app code. The model never decides which photo to show you; it only translates a sentence into a search, which keeps results predictable and explainable.

What it can and cannot find

Honest limits are more useful than a demo that only works in a keynote. TidyByte’s AI search covers visible content, and says so in the search field.

Works well for

Subjects and scenes: dogs, beaches, food, sunsets, cars, receipts, screenshots of documents, screens, and combinations like “dog on a beach” or “food, not screenshots”.

Not indexed here

Dates, place names, people’s names and text inside images. For those, the Photos app’s own search is the better tool, and TidyByte tells you that instead of returning nothing.

Search results behave like any other list in the app: select what you no longer need, export anything worth keeping, and delete the rest with one confirmation and a 30-day undo.

Why this is a privacy feature, not just a search box

Searching your photo library by description is the most personal query you can make of your own device. Doing it in the cloud means building a profile of what you photograph, when, and what you look for — which is exactly what on-device processing avoids.

TidyByte performs no network requests at all. There is no account, no analytics SDK, and no upload path for a photo or a search term. You can verify the claim by turning on airplane mode and searching, or by reading the source: it is MIT licensed on GitHub.

That is the point of building search this way: the feature that would normally be the most invasive one in a photo app becomes the one that never leaves the phone.
