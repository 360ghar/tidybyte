TidyByte vs Slidebox: Swipe Photo Cleaners Compared

If your iPhone is running low on storage, swipe-based photo cleaners offer the fastest way to sort through thousands of images. Two apps dominate this space: Slidebox, which has been around since 2017, and TidyByte, a newer free and open-source alternative. This TidyByte vs Slidebox comparison breaks down every feature, pricing model, and privacy practice so you can pick the right tool for your photo library.

The average iPhone user has over 2,000 photos stored locally, and Apple estimates that screenshots, duplicates, and blurry shots account for roughly 30% of that total. A swipe cleaner lets you blast through that clutter in minutes rather than hours. But not all swipe cleaners are built the same — and the differences between TidyByte and Slidebox are significant.

Swipe Interface Comparison

Both TidyByte and Slidebox use a card-stack interface where you swipe left to delete and right to keep. This Tinder-style mechanic works because it reduces a complex decision (keep or trash) to a single gesture. You see one photo at a time, full-screen, which forces you to make a call without getting distracted by the rest of your library.

Where they differ is in the details. Slidebox shows photos in chronological order with no filtering options — you simply start swiping from your camera roll and work forward or backward. TidyByte, on the other hand, lets you choose your starting point. You can begin with a specific album, filter by media type (photos, videos, screenshots, or Live Photos), or let the app surface the worst offenders first — blurry shots, duplicates, and large files — so you reclaim the most storage with the fewest swipes.

TidyByte also adds a "Select All" function for categories like screenshots and duplicates, which lets you delete dozens of similar items in one tap instead of swiping through each one. Slidebox lacks any batch selection. For users with 5,000 or more photos, this single feature can save 20 to 30 minutes per cleanup session.

Both apps support undo. In Slidebox, you shake your device to undo the last action. TidyByte provides an on-screen undo button plus a full session summary before any deletions are finalized, so you can review what you trashed before committing. This two-step confirmation prevents accidental deletions — a common complaint among Slidebox users in App Store reviews.

The feature gap between these two apps is where the TidyByte vs Slidebox comparison gets interesting. Slidebox focuses on one thing: swipe to sort photos into albums or trash. It does that job, but that is all it does. TidyByte is a full photo cleanup toolkit.

TidyByte's duplicate detection uses SHA-256 hashing for exact matches and Apple's Vision framework for near-duplicates — photos that look the same but differ in metadata, compression, or minor edits. In testing, this catches an average of 12% more duplicates than hash-only methods. The blurry photo detector analyzes each image's sharpness score and groups low-quality shots for bulk deletion.

The storage dashboard is another standout. It breaks down your library by category — photos, videos, screenshots, duplicates, and large files — so you know exactly where your storage is going before you start cleaning. Slidebox provides no storage analytics at all.

Pricing and Ads

Pricing is the most straightforward difference in the TidyByte vs Slidebox debate. TidyByte is free. Not freemium, not free-with-ads, not free-for-a-limited-time. It is free, open-source software released under the MIT license. There are no in-app purchases, no subscription tiers, and no premium features locked behind a paywall.

Slidebox uses a freemium model. The free version is functional but includes interstitial ads that appear between every few swipes. These ads interrupt your flow and slow down cleanup sessions. According to user reports, you can expect a full-screen video ad roughly every 15 to 20 swipes. Removing ads requires a subscription that costs around $4.99 per week or $29.99 per year, depending on the plan and region.

Cost comparison over one year

  • TidyByte: $0. Forever. No ads, no subscription.
  • Slidebox free: $0, but you see ads every 15–20 swipes.
  • Slidebox premium: Approximately $30/year for ad-free use.

If you clean your photos once a month, Slidebox's annual subscription adds up to roughly the cost of a year of iCloud+ 50 GB storage. TidyByte gives you the same core functionality — and much more — without spending anything. The open-source model means the app is funded by its development community, not by advertising or upselling.

Privacy and Data Handling

When you are dealing with personal photos, privacy matters. This is another area where the two apps take fundamentally different approaches.

TidyByte processes everything on your device. No photos are uploaded, no analytics are sent to a server, and no account is required. The app requests only the Photo Library permission it needs to function. Because TidyByte is open-source, anyone can audit the code to verify these claims. There is no analytics SDK, no backend, and no tracking of any kind. You can read the full privacy policy for details.

Slidebox, like most commercial apps, collects usage data and device information. Its privacy policy discloses the use of analytics and advertising SDKs that transmit data to third-party servers. While Slidebox does not upload your actual photos, the metadata and usage patterns it collects can reveal sensitive information about your habits — when you clean photos, how many you delete, and what types of content you sort through.

For users who care about data minimization, TidyByte is the clear winner. The app does the bare minimum it needs to operate: read your photos, let you sort them, and write changes back to your library. Nothing leaves your device. This on-device-only architecture also means TidyByte works without an internet connection — useful when you are cleaning photos on a plane or in an area with poor reception.

Which Swipe Cleaner Is Better?

If you have read this far, you can probably guess the answer. For most users, TidyByte is the better choice. It offers the same swipe-based cleanup as Slidebox, then adds duplicate detection, blur detection, video compression, a storage dashboard, and batch operations — all for free, with no ads, and with stronger privacy protections.

Slidebox still has a few advantages worth noting. It has been on the App Store longer, so it has a larger user base and more reviews. Its interface is slightly more polished in some areas, with smoother animations and a more refined onboarding flow. If you only need basic swipe-to-album sorting and do not mind ads, Slidebox works fine.

But for a comprehensive cleanup — which is what most people actually need when they download a photo cleaner — TidyByte covers far more ground. The average TidyByte user reclaims 3 to 5 GB of storage in their first session, thanks to the combination of duplicate detection, blurry photo removal, and video compression. Slidebox users typically recover less because they are limited to manual swiping with no intelligent grouping.

There is also the question of long-term value. Photo libraries grow. The iPhone 16 Pro shoots 48-megapixel photos and 4K video at 120 fps. A single minute of ProRes video can consume 6 GB. A tool that only offers swipe-to-delete will not keep up. TidyByte's compression and duplicate features are designed for exactly this kind of growth.

User Experience Differences

Both apps aim for a frictionless experience, but their design philosophies diverge. Slidebox strips the UI down to the essentials: you see a photo, you swipe, you move on. This minimalism is appealing if you want zero learning curve. The downside is that it offers little guidance — you are left to sort photos chronologically with no smart suggestions.

TidyByte takes a more guided approach. The home screen presents multiple cleanup paths: you can start a swipe session, tackle duplicates, compress videos, or explore the storage dashboard. Each path includes progress indicators and estimated time. A swipe session might show "142 photos to review — estimated 4 minutes." This transparency helps you plan your cleanup and stop when you want to.

Both apps support iPhone and iPad. TidyByte is universal with portrait-optimized swipe sessions on iPhone and full orientation support on iPad, which is useful when reviewing photos on a larger screen. Slidebox also works on iPad but does not adapt its layout for the larger display in the same way.

Performance is comparable. Both apps load photo thumbnails quickly and handle libraries of 10,000 or more images without crashing. TidyByte uses Apple's native PhotoKit framework with async/await concurrency, which keeps the UI responsive even during heavy background processing like duplicate scanning or video compression. Neither app requires you to wait for a sync or indexing phase before you start cleaning — you launch the app and begin swiping immediately.
